A client recently told me:

> “Wow, we just got out of the meeting and you already shared the updated proposal with the points we talked about.”

That speed did not come from writing faster or rushing through the follow-up.

It came from making our strategy, proposal patterns, website, delivery playbooks, company documentation, and operating rules part of the same shared context.

With the right MCP connections, I can leave a meeting, work from what we discussed, draft the proposal, apply a proven skill, update the relevant website content, and share the result while the conversation is still fresh.

Most services businesses restart from zero more often than they realize.

Every new employee receives another version of the same explanation. Every proposal begins as another blank document. Every completed project traps useful knowledge inside that delivery. Every marketing asset recreates patterns the company already solved.

I wanted each piece of work to make the next one easier.

Organizing company knowledge so AI can use it is becoming a common pattern. It helps a founder find information, preserve context, and work faster.

I think the larger opportunity is organizational: making the same context useful to your team, your agents, your operating systems, and eventually your customers.

On my computer, I keep one main workspace containing the systems behind my company. It includes our business data, GTM strategy, internal documentation, reusable skills, public website, operating tools, and video-production projects. The parts remain independently versioned, but they are close enough to share context.

This has changed onboarding and alignment.

My lead-generation contractor can read our private GTM site and understand our positioning, offers, ideal customers, strategic priorities, numbers, and boundaries. He does not only receive a list of leads to find. He understands the strategy that should guide his judgment.

More than 20 developers have worked with me over time. New developers can visit [docs.fzf.dev](https://docs.fzf.dev) and read what I would otherwise need to teach each person repeatedly: how we communicate, estimate work, protect client budgets, use AI responsibly, own technical decisions, report time, and deliver quality work.

When I notice a recurring problem or change an expectation, I update the relevant document. Starlight turns the source into a documentation site, and Cloudflare makes the new version privately available to the team.

Documentation does not replace leadership. It means leadership no longer has to begin every conversation from zero.

The same structure also guides agents.

At the top of the workspace, an `AGENTS.md` file explains the company-wide context and rules. The instructions are recursive, so any directory can have its own `AGENTS.md` with guidance for that area.

The GTM directory can add commercial rules. The documentation directory can define how company knowledge should be written. A client project can add its specific constraints and validation requirements.

Context becomes more specific as the agent moves closer to the work, while the broader company principles still apply. It resembles healthy delegation: shared direction at the company level and specialized judgment closer to the task.

Client delivery compounds too.

When we solve a new client problem, I look for the pattern underneath the implementation. I remove the client-specific details and capture the reusable knowledge in a focused skill containing the workflow, references, and lessons we want the next agent to inherit.

The client receives the outcome they paid for. We keep a more capable delivery system for the next engagement.

We are not copying client solutions. We are extracting reusable operating knowledge.

The same thing happens in marketing. HyperFrames holds shared production patterns, assets, narration, and composition rules, so every new video starts from a stronger system instead of another blank timeline.

Our metrics complete the loop. The business data has one consistent model for clients, projects, commercial terms, rates, hours, and delivery health. I or an agent can inspect revenue, project economics, billing, contractor payables, and project risk without creating another disconnected version of the truth.

This is what I find most powerful about shared context.

A decision improves the documentation. The documentation improves how people and agents work. Their work produces better data and new delivery knowledge. That knowledge becomes another skill, tool, policy, or media pattern. The next person or agent begins from a stronger company.

Organizing files for AI is becoming a common productivity pattern. The next advantage is not having more files. It is shortening the distance between learning something and the whole company acting on it.

How much of your company’s knowledge compounds—and how much disappears into meetings, chats, and completed projects?
